Chapter 10 

 

Teddy spurred his horse north along the road, which was nothing more than a narrow cattle trail.  Kiah rode to his brothers left while the other riders fell in line, forming a typical cavalry traveling column, two by two.   

 

William and his companions watched the riders disappear around a slight bend.  William turned to them, his face flushed with anger.  Hissing through clenched teeth he declared, “This does not end here.” 

 

Flurries began falling fifteen minutes into the journey carpeting the countryside with a thick heavy snow looking like a homemade bedspread used to keep the winter chill at bay.  A content smile spread across Teddy’s rugged face.  Turning towards his son Teddy proclaimed, “Need more of this, Wulf.  Quiets the hooves.”  

 

Wulf ​​shook his head in agreement.   

 

The riders journeyed in silence as unnecessary chatter might alert their prey.  They followed the trail winding through Blackhawk’s northern hill country for the next hour.  Wulf knew the northern frontier of the territory well.  He frequently hunted these lands with his father, uncle, Seth or, with other boys from the village, on occasion. 

 

Despite the snow the riders made steady progress north.  At an intersection with another trail coming in from the south west Kiah held up his hand.  Dismounting his horse, he knelt in the mud and snow tracing the tracks with his finger.  Kiah closed his eyes, allowing his senses to reach out searching for the orcs.  He smirked looking up and explained, “Fresh orc tracks.  They head north.  At least twenty.  Moving fast but looks like they are loaded down.  We are only an hour maybe slightly less behind.”  

 

Kiah climbed back into the saddle giving Wulf a wink and smile.  Thirty minutes later Teddy stopped the patrol at the foot of a slight rise.  Reaching for his longbow Teddy ordered, “Kiah, Wulf, steal a look.  Horses getting edgy.  We’re close.” 

 

Kiah and Wulf grabbed their longbows then scrambled through the sparse pines towards the top of the rise.  About halfway up Kiah stopped and gave Wulf a curious glance.  “What’s jingling?”  Are you wearing chain under your leather armor?” 

 

Trying to avoid his uncle’s gaze Wulf replied sheepishly, “Yes.” 

 

Trying not to laugh, Kiah asked, “Your Mom?”   

 

“​​Yes,” Wulf muttered. 

 

“Ahh.  Well, they’re like that,” Kiah offered with an understanding grin and a reassuring nod.  “Come on.”  

 

The two Blackhawks resumed their climb.  Kiah dropped to his belly about five feet from the summit and Wulf followed his uncle’s lead.  From the crest they obtained a view of the narrow river basin below.  Kiah pointed towards the orcs about ​​five hundred yards ahead.  “There they are my ​​boy!  Look, I count over a dozen.  Good hunting today.” 

 

Kiah and Wulf hustled their way back to the waiting riders, hoist themselves into their saddles.  Looking at this brother, Kiah provided an update, “They’re five hundred yards north of us.  We should catch them before crossing the North.” 

 

Teddy nodded spurring his horse forward, the others following his lead.  The trail exited the hill country into the North River basin.  The basin consisted of a broad flat plain dotted with stunted oaks and knotted brush. Offering little cover or concealment much like the Border Lands located north of the river.  Venturing into the Border Lands with thirteen riders, would prove foolish so the orcs must be slain before they reach the north ​​shore.   

 

The orcs came into full view just short of the river’s south bank each carrying a large gunny sack slung across their shoulders.  Orcs ambled with a hunched or bent posture like an old man in need of a cane, suffered hoggish faces, and low sloped brows. Like some hideous creature from a child’s nightmare.  Grotesque or misshapen more aptly defined their appearance.  They wore a crude patchwork of hide and leather ​​armor.   

 

The orcs did not notice the riders burst from the ​​hills.  As the terrain broadened, the riders transitioned from a traveling column to a single ​​battleline.  Teddy took the center with Wulf at his immediate right allowing his sword arm to protect his son.  Bull maneuvered to Teddy’s other side, prepared to lead the left wing of the attack.  Teddy nodded in approval, acknowledging Bull’s continued employment of Crawsterian cavalry tactics.  Ever the ranger, Kiah positioned himself at the end of the left flank to support the charge with his bow. 

 

Hearing hooves pounding the frozen ground caught the orcs attention as they stepped into the North ​​River.  Orcs in the rear shouted a warning, alerting the others.  Surprised, the orcs turn to steel themselves against the threat.  They appeared surprised, caught off guard, and not expecting retaliation especially this close to the Border Lands.  Those carrying the loot draw their short swords while others nock arrows in their short bows.   

 

Teddy slowed his horse slightly taken aback at the boldness of these orcs.  No orcs he encountered in these parts ever turned to fight with riders galloping towards them.  He was even startled observing one act as a leader, directing the orcs into defensive ​​positions.   

 

Kiah recognized what seemed to be a defensive formation employed during the Goblin Wars.  He reigned his horse hard to the left, crossing the North to deter any single orc from fleeing.  Teddy grinned slyly as Kiah splashed into the river.  ‘He saw the same thing.’ 

 

Teddy signaled Bull and his riders to follow Kiah as the orc archers took aim.  Bull and five horsemen ride hard, turning directly north fording the North River short of the orc position.   

      

Teddy ordered the attack while releasing the arrow nocked in his longbow.  The ​​arrow struck an orc, pierced its leather armor.  The orc dropped along the bank.   

 

One down.   

 

Wulf let an arrow fly.   

 

Two down.   

 

The remaining riders along the south bank let loose, killing more orcs in the river’s shallow waters.   

 

The orcs realized they were no match for the attacking riders.  They panicked despite the furious frantic urgings of their apparent ​​leader.  The orcs became entangled with each other while desperately trying to avoid the deadly missiles raining down on them.   

 

Kiah, Bull, and the riders along the north bank unleashed a volley of arrows cutting down more orcs and trapped the remaining between the two riverbanks.   

 

The orc leader stood a head above the other orcs.  He remained calm despite the carnage around him as if this were not his first skirmish with men.  Sneering, he took aim.  His arrow found its target. Sending Teddy and his mount tumbling into the river.  Howling in delight, the orc leader placed another arrow into the short bow.   

 

Wulf observed his father and horse plunge into the icy water.  Kiah and Bull watched in horror as Wulf urged Maggie into the river without hesitation.   

 

They shouted in unison, “Wulf, no!” 

 

Wulf drew Ill Wind while continuing a furious charge towards the large orc.  The orc scoffed while aiming his bow at Wulf.  His arrow grazed the side of Wulf’s neck above the shoulder padding, drawing blood.  The slicing wound forced a grimace.  The pain from the arrow was quickly replaced by the shock of Maggie hitting the cold water at full stride.   

 

Wulf shrugged off the discomfort, urging Maggie forward, faster.  He closed the distance before the orc could fire again.  Nearing his target Wulf launched himself into the leader.  His shoulder connected with the orc’s chest. Breaking bone. Driving air from the lungs.  The violent collision sent both into the shallow nearly frozen river.   

 

Wulf gained his feet. Time decelerated. Everything seemed to move in slow motion.  The stunned orc floundered on his back. Grasping for breath. Groping for his sword.  Wulf gathered himself. Raised his sword. Drove the blade deep into the orc leader’s chest before it could recover.  The last handful of orcs rallied to their fallen leader. Hissing and growling as they cautiously approached Wulf.  Arrows dropped three. Two orcs closest to Wulf pressed their attack.  Wulf removed Ill Wind from the dead orc. Turned to face his assailants.   

 

“Strong, but clumsy,” Wulf mouthed.  “Move fast.  Strike hard.  And show no fear.” 

 

Wulf parried the first orc’s wild swing.  The clash of the swords drove the attacking orc back. Nearly knocking him off his feet.  Wulf instinctively shifted to his right as the second orc over-committed with an errant sword thrust.  Wulf swung the ancient two-handed blade slicing flesh and cracking ribs.  His opponent crumpled into the river.  The first orc recovered and moved for another attack while Wulf remained distracted.  Sensing the threat, Wulf pivoted to face the remaining orc whose short sword was raised above his head.  Wulf intuitively hoisted his sword to meet an attack that never comes.   

 

Teddy’s arrow pierced the back of the orc’s skull before it could complete the swing.  Slightly annoyed, Wulf back stepped to avoid the falling orc.  Wulf wiped the bloodied blade on his water-soaked cloak while staring at the dead orcs laying at his feet.  He sheathed his sword before heading towards his father.   

 

“Your mother will kill me…,” Teddy muttered while examining the arrow wound on his son’s ​​neck.  “That’s a tough one to hide.  Doesn’t look too bad, though.”  

 

Teddy, Wulf, and his father’s wounded warhorse slowly lumbered their way out of the creek towards the remaining riders waiting for them.  Always loyal, ​​Maggie snorted and pawed impatiently at the mud waiting for Wulf.   

 

Kiah proudly smiled at Wulf as he reigned his horse to a halt along the riverbank.  He dismounted then looked at his brother with a slight grin.  “Oh, Helen will not like this.”   

 

Teddy grunted as Kiah waded towards the fallen orcs.  Bull and the others established defensive potions along the river’s north bank while Kiah examined the orc leader.  Kiah knelt, ran his gloved hand over the dead orc’s armor. Studied his face.  ‘Something is different about this one, something’s not quite right.  He seems almost semi-human.’  Kiah stood staring.  The water around the dead had turned red from the orc blood.  ‘How much blood would be shed along this river in the coming days or even ​​years?’ 

 

“Moss take three riders home then bring back additional horses to help carry the valuables,” Teddy ordered.   

 

“On our way,” Moss replied.   

 

“Wulf, you are reckless,” Bull bellowed approvingly along with a slap on the back.  

 

Wulf nodded towards Bull and gave him a slight, almost embarrassed smile.  Wulf cradled Maggie’s head in his hands then stroked her ​​neck.  He whispered something into her ear, and she replied with a hushed whinny.   

 

The remaining riders were speechless not because they doubted Teddy’s decision to bring Wulf. which they did, because of the young Blackhawk’s prowess.  The former ​​soldiers stared at Wulf in disbelief and simply shook their heads without uttering a word.  Kiah watched their reaction then laughed to himself.  ‘The boy showed no fear.’ 

 

​​​Around mid-afternoon the riders entered Shallow Cross each going their separate ways, each with tales to tell about their adventure along the Border Lands.  Teddy and Wulf rode across the Kinnereth River bridge arriving home a few minutes later.  Despite the cold, Helen and Seth waited for them on the front porch. 

 

“Well, here we go,” Teddy expressed with a slight smile while looking at his son.  “There’s going to be hell to pay.” 

 

Wulf returned the smile cautiously as he watched his mother running towards him.

For others, a skirmish along the Border Lands.

I did not detect any others.”

Chapter 2 (Deemos, the Master, and Aeros Fall 531) 

 
Slipping from the shadows, the drow maiden appeared as a wraith, shaded, and menacing. 

“He is here,” she hissed. Her slender features veiled beneath her cloak; her face obscured by the folds of her cowl.  

Jaisia’s sudden presence startled Deemos. He gathered himself and stood. He towered over her, yet she did not flinch.. Deemos fixed his calloused eyes on her. “You were not gone long.” 

“I was easy,” she confessed, shrugging her shoulders. “I followed a trail of ale.” 

“Is he sober?” 

“Hard to tell,” she admitted, her voice hard and toneless. “He smells.” 

“No matter,” Deemos chuckled, low and wicked. “The kobold will meet the Master now.”  

 

◊ ◊ ◊ 

 

Deemos suffered Belhaaz’ impatience. Rhythmic tapping from the talons grew louder indicating a building frustration. Belhaaz was anxious to capture his heir after decades of searching. As the war raged upon the Southern Realms, Deemos was exasperated by this enduring infatuation to apprehend a child Belhaaz never met. He failed to grasp the child’s importance. Deemos yearned for Belhaaz’ complete concentration on prosecuting the war not locating a long-lost daughter. Deemos desired for Belhaaz to personally lead the armies positioned to deliver knockout blows against the Highlands and Beth Amen. Once the barbarians and Horse Tribes were destroyed, the master’s forces would lay waste to the rest of the Southern Realms nations. Deemos paused near the dais until Belhaaz summoned him on. 

Belhaaz lifted his gaze to meet Deemos’. “Aeros?” 

 “Yes, my Master,” Deemos confirmed, his bow low . 

Deemos heard the master exhale in satisfaction, saw his yellow eyes flicker in gratification. “Bring him in.”

“Reload! Fire on your own!” Bahrmiore shouted. “Darrishaw, brace yourselves.”

“Uggruk.” Sorothrash boldly strode from the forest, his sword hoisted above his head. His assault on Wingrove faltered. The devastating impact of crossbow bolts reduced his command by half. Sorothrash halted in the center of the clearing as if daring a soldier to shoot him. “Get moving, rabble. Commanders, press the attack.”

The ramparts shuddered when a mass of orcs crashed into the weakened wall. Orc blades hacked and slashed at the soldiers defending the village. Soldiers retreated and drew the orcs deeper into Wingrove to further thin their ranks.

Bahrmiore spied the half-orc and trembled. Like Prokrupp, Sorothrash was a monster spawned in the depths of the abyss. Stamm understood his limits with a sword, and crossing blades with the enormous half orc would result in his death. He needed to slay the half-orc from a distance. Stamm armed a discarded crossbow. He aimed at Sorothrash, exhaled, and squeezed the trigger.

Sorothrash flinched as the bolt bored into his side. He snatched the bolt and yanked it from his body.

Stamm rearmed the crossbow and fired again. Sorothrash stuttered on his next step. The bolt had penetrated his armor and punctured his heart. For a moment, a searing heat engulfed his entire body. The half orc staggered then collapsed.

◊ ◊ ◊

The sound of the healers’ screams filled Laniken’s ears, a haunting reminder of their tragic demise on orc blades. He perceived the boots scraping over the stone floor and heard the orc’s labored breathing and their guttural grunts. His eyes flew open; the orcs hovered above him, their swords glimmering in the torchlight. In an instant, everything went black.

◊ ◊ ◊

Stamm reloaded his crossbow and checked the half orc again. Sorothrash did not move. He’s dead. Stamm leaned over the ramparts and emptied his stomach. He wiped his mouth and surveyed the village. In the streets and alleyways, humans and orcs ripped at each other with teeth, claws, knives, and swords. Along the walls and inside the buildings, they wrestled for control and fought to survive. For hours, steel clanged against steel. Blades cleaved flesh and spilled blood. Orcs and men died, with neither gaining an advantage.

By mid-morning, the battle finally culminated in its horrific conclusion. Somehow, Stamm’s soldiers won. Orcs and men lay dead, their bodies entangled in a gruesome embrace. He discovered Darrishaw’s lifeless body among a mass of dead near the east wall. Stamm trekked through the wrecked village, searching for survivors. Stamm entered the infirmary and gazed at the grisly sight. Dead healers and patients littered the floor, their bodies hacked apart by orc swords. He found Laniken’s body and grimaced. Laniken lay on his cot, his blood dripping onto the stone below. Stamm departed the infirmary knowing what remained of his forces must evacuate; they no longer possessed the ability to defend the village or themselves. “Sergeant, gather our remnant near the north gate. We march for Fairbriar in an hour.”

◊ ◊ ◊

Seth rose from the damp ground and stretched, relieving the stiffness in his tired muscles. His awareness dispersed; an invisible force exploring the wild and probing the village. Twenty-four humans inhabited the village, and his patrol flanked the Wingrove Cut, obstructing any route north. The ranger narrowed his search, focusing on the orcs. He no longer detected a single orc. Had the enemy soldiers killed all the orcs?

Seth shouldered his pack and extra quiver, then proceeded to the village. Based on the sun’s position, noon would arrive in two hours.

◊ ◊ ◊

Throughout the sunrise hour and into the morning, Lieutenant Badeaux and his patrol listened to the fighting inside Wingrove. A cacophony of shouts and screams rising from the village echoed in their ears. From their vantage point beyond the village’s wall, the Brigade soldiers strained to discern who prevailed. The battle abruptly concluded around midmorning, and a foreboding silence ensued.

Rook tilted his head to the side, listening for any sounds of battle.

“Is it over?” Candor whispered; his attention glued to the village.

“Not sure,” Rook admitted. “I can’t hear anything.”

Fifty feet from Candor and Rook, Cade and Band perceived the same unsettling stillness that hung in the air.

“Band, shift us slightly east of the Wingrove Cut,” Cade ordered, surveying the village. “Arrange the patrol opposite the north gate. We will engage any force that exits.”

“Aye.”

Within thirty minutes, weary Brigade soldiers occupied a new location a dozen yards inside the forest.

◊ ◊ ◊


Shrouded in his arcana, Seth reentered Wingrove through a broad gap in the east wall and witnessed a gruesome scene. A harsh wind blew in from the northwest and he tasted burnt wood. Fire ruined the east wall, now nothing more than weakened charcoal slats. Devoid of any sound or commotion, Wingrove had become a graveyard for the unburied. Orcs and men lay strewn in the streets and alleyways, their corpses entangled in a macabre dance. The earth within the walls glistened in the morning sun like rain had fallen, yet the liquid was not transparent, but a dark red. The sickening aroma emanating from the dead assaulted his nose. Blood pooled on the stone and gore burnished the walls. Death reigned over the village, and the survivors grappled with their fear.

◊ ◊ ◊

Lieutenant Stamm Bahrmiore arrived at the north gate within an hour and failed to hide his disappointment. “Sergeant, this is all?”

“Sir, we scoured the village for survivors, and twenty-four is what we have left.”

Stamm’s stomach churned, gazing at what remained. Two days of fighting had reduced the company to a mere twenty-four soldiers and nearly half sustained injury. “Open the gate.”

Iron hinges protested and wood beams groaned as the gate opened. Stamm ordered his fatigued soldiers forward to begin their two-day journey back to the garrison at Fairbriar.

Stamm pondered his explanation to his battalion commander upon his arrival in Fairbriar. How did he justify the slaughter of Laniken’s platoon or the day long barrage from an unknown assailant or assailants? Finally, the orc attack. He widely believed that the orcs possessed an unwavering loyalty, but to everyone’s astonishment, Sorothrash betrayed them, leaving behind a trail of confusion and unanswered questions. Prokrupp brought this on us. His heavy-handed leadership caused the orcs to rebel. Yes, pin the blame on Prokrupp. That’s my story, he concluded.

◊ ◊ ◊

The ranger perceived voices to the north, heard the gate’s iron hinges protest. Seth leapt over the dead and prepared his bow on the run. He slowed and peered around the corner. The enemy soldiers marched from the village in two columns. Seth snarled; the hunt had resumed.

Seth selected his target and exhaled. The string thudded when he released his arrow. The soldier grabbed his comrade’s arm. His knees buckled and stepped once more, then fell face forward onto the ground. The ranger rearmed his bow and progressed from one target to another; each precise shot found a gap in the armor. Every arrow produced the same deadly results.

◊ ◊ ◊


Concealed within the forest, Lieutenant Badeaux observed the north gate open and a column of soldiers march from Wingrove. He nocked an arrow, aimed at the lead soldier, then wavered. Their heads hung and their shoulders slumped. They appeared worn, defeated. For a moment, he considered these soldiers human and believed they had suffered enough. Cade glanced around; the wanton devastation of his homeland filled his vision. Destruction was evident wherever his gaze landed. This is war. Cade spat; vengeance replaced sympathy. Cade refocused and a slight grin creased his face. Use the bow first. At the natural release point, he let his arrow fly. His Brigade soldiers mimicked his action and freed their arrows. In an instant, seventeen arrows sliced the chilled air. Fletchings hissed in anger as they sped towards their targets.

◊ ◊ ◊

Lieutenant Stamm Bahrmiore snapped his head when he heard his soldiers screaming behind him. He placed his right hand on the hilt of his sword, then gagged. As his fingers groped for the arrow piercing his side, he spun. A soldier to his left collapsed with an arrow buried in his neck. Another soldier near him huffed and then crumpled to the earth; the arrow had punctured his lung. Stamm wiped the blood from his mouth and sagged to his knees. He fought to remain conscious. He retched; his vision blurred. Lieutenant Stamm Bahrmiore bled out, his blood a scarlet blemish upon Blackhawk soil.

◊ ◊ ◊

Bow armed, Seth picked his way through the dead, his awareness sweeping the village. No orcs, no soldiers lived. He sensed nothing else in the vicinity except his patrol and Western Union. The ranger eased his draw and returned the arrow to his quiver. He withdrew the cowl from his head; the morning sun had banished the chill, and he savored its warmth on his skin. Seth met Cade and the Brigade soldiers at the north gate. “Gentlemen, welcome. Wingrove is now under new management.”

“By the Maiden, captain,” Cade smirked. “If I did not personally witness the destruction here, I would never consider this possible. You wiped—”

“No,” Seth interrupted. “We, the Brigade, won this victory.”

Cade did not reply while his brain tried to grasp the carnage.

"Cade, ask Band, Fynn, and Rook,” Seth proclaimed. He clapped his hand onto Cade’s shoulder and motioned towards the ruined village. “These are the results you achieve when you take the fight to the enemy. This is how we kept the orcs at bay in Beth Amen.”

“What’s next, captain?” Band asked.

“Search the village for anything useful,” Seth began. “Then find shelter. I think it best we rest before heading to Fairbriar.”

The Brigade soldiers gaped at Seth while he stepped past Cade and Band and headed out the gate.

“Captain, where are you going?” Fynn inquired.

Seth did not stop. “Western Union.”

“Ah, quit yer blasted gawken!” Band barked. “You heard the captain. Scour the village for anything useful.”

◊ ◊ ◊

Seth found Western Union drinking from a stream and he admired from afar her. He loved this magnificent animal. With her distinctive coat and steadfast loyalty, Western Union was exceptional in every aspect. She displayed immense courage and never flinched when they rode into battle. Western Union was a warrior, and like him, her body bore the scars from many skirmishes. Seth marveled at her. Smart and durable, Western Union was the perfect ranger’s horse, and his sole surviving connection to Kiah.

Western Union’s head gently raised and her ears twitched when she heard him approach. She snorted and stomped her hoof.

“I missed you too.” Seth opened his arms, and she walked into his embrace. For a long moment he held Western Union close, and, in an instant, his anger leached from his body. “There is more work that needs to be done. Ready?”
She whinnied in reply.
