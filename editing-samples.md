The Outlanders: The Legend of Blackhawk 

 

Chapter 10 

 

Teddy spurred his horse north along the road, which was nothing more than a narrow cattle trail.  Kiah rode to his brothers left while the other riders fell in line, forming a typical cavalry traveling column, two by two.   

 

William and his companions watched the riders disappear around a slight bend.  William turned to them, his face flushed with anger.  Hissing through clenched teeth he declared, “This does not end here.” 

 

Flurries began falling fifteen minutes into the journey carpeting the countryside with a thick heavy snow looking like a homemade bedspread used to keep the winter chill at bay.  A content smile spread across Teddy’s rugged face.  Turning towards his son Teddy proclaimed, “Need more of this, Wulf.  Quiets the hooves.”  

 

Wulf ​​shook his head in agreement.   

 

The riders journeyed in silence as unnecessary chatter might alert their prey.  They followed the trail winding through Blackhawk’s northern hill country for the next hour.  Wulf knew the northern frontier of the territory well.  He frequently hunted these lands with his father, uncle, Seth or, with other boys from the village, on occasion. 

 

Despite the snow the riders made steady progress north.  At an intersection with another trail coming in from the south west Kiah held up his hand.  Dismounting his horse, he knelt in the mud and snow tracing the tracks with his finger.  Kiah closed his eyes, allowing his senses to reach out searching for the orcs.  He smirked looking up and explained, “Fresh orc tracks.  They head north.  At least twenty.  Moving fast but looks like they are loaded down.  We are only an hour maybe slightly less behind.”  

 

Kiah climbed back into the saddle giving Wulf a wink and smile.  Thirty minutes later Teddy stopped the patrol at the foot of a slight rise.  Reaching for his longbow Teddy ordered, “Kiah, Wulf, steal a look.  Horses getting edgy.  We’re close.” 

 

Kiah and Wulf grabbed their longbows then scrambled through the sparse pines towards the top of the rise.  About halfway up Kiah stopped and gave Wulf a curious glance.  “What’s jingling?”  Are you wearing chain under your leather armor?” 

 

Trying to avoid his uncle’s gaze Wulf replied sheepishly, “Yes.” 

 

Trying not to laugh, Kiah asked, “Your Mom?”   

 

“​​Yes,” Wulf muttered. 

 

“Ahh.  Well, they’re like that,” Kiah offered with an understanding grin and a reassuring nod.  “Come on.”  

 

The two Blackhawks resumed their climb.  Kiah dropped to his belly about five feet from the summit and Wulf followed his uncle’s lead.  From the crest they obtained a view of the narrow river basin below.  Kiah pointed towards the orcs about ​​five hundred yards ahead.  “There they are my ​​boy!  Look, I count over a dozen.  Good hunting today.” 

 

Kiah and Wulf hustled their way back to the waiting riders, hoist themselves into their saddles.  Looking at this brother, Kiah provided an update, “They’re five hundred yards north of us.  We should catch them before crossing the North.” 

 

Teddy nodded spurring his horse forward, the others following his lead.  The trail exited the hill country into the North River basin.  The basin consisted of a broad flat plain dotted with stunted oaks and knotted brush. Offering little cover or concealment much like the Border Lands located north of the river.  Venturing into the Border Lands with thirteen riders, would prove foolish so the orcs must be slain before they reach the north ​​shore.   

 

The orcs came into full view just short of the river’s south bank each carrying a large gunny sack slung across their shoulders.  Orcs ambled with a hunched or bent posture like an old man in need of a cane, suffered hoggish faces, and low sloped brows. Like some hideous creature from a child’s nightmare.  Grotesque or misshapen more aptly defined their appearance.  They wore a crude patchwork of hide and leather ​​armor.   

 

The orcs did not notice the riders burst from the ​​hills.  As the terrain broadened, the riders transitioned from a traveling column to a single ​​battleline.  Teddy took the center with Wulf at his immediate right allowing his sword arm to protect his son.  Bull maneuvered to Teddy’s other side, prepared to lead the left wing of the attack.  Teddy nodded in approval, acknowledging Bull’s continued employment of Crawsterian cavalry tactics.  Ever the ranger, Kiah positioned himself at the end of the left flank to support the charge with his bow. 

 

Hearing hooves pounding the frozen ground caught the orcs attention as they stepped into the North ​​River.  Orcs in the rear shouted a warning, alerting the others.  Surprised, the orcs turn to steel themselves against the threat.  They appeared surprised, caught off guard, and not expecting retaliation especially this close to the Border Lands.  Those carrying the loot draw their short swords while others nock arrows in their short bows.   

 

Teddy slowed his horse slightly taken aback at the boldness of these orcs.  No orcs he encountered in these parts ever turned to fight with riders galloping towards them.  He was even startled observing one act as a leader, directing the orcs into defensive ​​positions.   

 

Kiah recognized what seemed to be a defensive formation employed during the Goblin Wars.  He reigned his horse hard to the left, crossing the North to deter any single orc from fleeing.  Teddy grinned slyly as Kiah splashed into the river.  ‘He saw the same thing.’ 

 

Teddy signaled Bull and his riders to follow Kiah as the orc archers took aim.  Bull and five horsemen ride hard, turning directly north fording the North River short of the orc position.   

      

Teddy ordered the attack while releasing the arrow nocked in his longbow.  The ​​arrow struck an orc, pierced its leather armor.  The orc dropped along the bank.   

 

One down.   

 

Wulf let an arrow fly.   

 

Two down.   

 

The remaining riders along the south bank let loose, killing more orcs in the river’s shallow waters.   

 

The orcs realized they were no match for the attacking riders.  They panicked despite the furious frantic urgings of their apparent ​​leader.  The orcs became entangled with each other while desperately trying to avoid the deadly missiles raining down on them.   

 

Kiah, Bull, and the riders along the north bank unleashed a volley of arrows cutting down more orcs and trapped the remaining between the two riverbanks.   

 

The orc leader stood a head above the other orcs.  He remained calm despite the carnage around him as if this were not his first skirmish with men.  Sneering, he took aim.  His arrow found its target. Sending Teddy and his mount tumbling into the river.  Howling in delight, the orc leader placed another arrow into the short bow.   

 

Wulf observed his father and horse plunge into the icy water.  Kiah and Bull watched in horror as Wulf urged Maggie into the river without hesitation.   

 

They shouted in unison, “Wulf, no!” 

 

Wulf drew Ill Wind while continuing a furious charge towards the large orc.  The orc scoffed while aiming his bow at Wulf.  His arrow grazed the side of Wulf’s neck above the shoulder padding, drawing blood.  The slicing wound forced a grimace.  The pain from the arrow was quickly replaced by the shock of Maggie hitting the cold water at full stride.   

 

Wulf shrugged off the discomfort, urging Maggie forward, faster.  He closed the distance before the orc could fire again.  Nearing his target Wulf launched himself into the leader.  His shoulder connected with the orc’s chest. Breaking bone. Driving air from the lungs.  The violent collision sent both into the shallow nearly frozen river.   

 

Wulf gained his feet. Time decelerated. Everything seemed to move in slow motion.  The stunned orc floundered on his back. Grasping for breath. Groping for his sword.  Wulf gathered himself. Raised his sword. Drove the blade deep into the orc leader’s chest before it could recover.  The last handful of orcs rallied to their fallen leader. Hissing and growling as they cautiously approached Wulf.  Arrows dropped three. Two orcs closest to Wulf pressed their attack.  Wulf removed Ill Wind from the dead orc. Turned to face his assailants.   

 

“Strong, but clumsy,” Wulf mouthed.  “Move fast.  Strike hard.  And show no fear.” 

 

Wulf parried the first orc’s wild swing.  The clash of the swords drove the attacking orc back. Nearly knocking him off his feet.  Wulf instinctively shifted to his right as the second orc over-committed with an errant sword thrust.  Wulf swung the ancient two-handed blade slicing flesh and cracking ribs.  His opponent crumpled into the river.  The first orc recovered and moved for another attack while Wulf remained distracted.  Sensing the threat, Wulf pivoted to face the remaining orc whose short sword was raised above his head.  Wulf intuitively hoisted his sword to meet an attack that never comes.   

 

Teddy’s arrow pierced the back of the orc’s skull before it could complete the swing.  Slightly annoyed, Wulf back stepped to avoid the falling orc.  Wulf wiped the bloodied blade on his water-soaked cloak while staring at the dead orcs laying at his feet.  He sheathed his sword before heading towards his father.   

 

“Your mother will kill me…,” Teddy muttered while examining the arrow wound on his son’s ​​neck.  “That’s a tough one to hide.  Doesn’t look too bad, though.”  

 

Teddy, Wulf, and his father’s wounded warhorse slowly lumbered their way out of the creek towards the remaining riders waiting for them.  Always loyal, ​​Maggie snorted and pawed impatiently at the mud waiting for Wulf.   

 

Kiah proudly smiled at Wulf as he reigned his horse to a halt along the riverbank.  He dismounted then looked at his brother with a slight grin.  “Oh, Helen will not like this.”   

 

Teddy grunted as Kiah waded towards the fallen orcs.  Bull and the others established defensive potions along the river’s north bank while Kiah examined the orc leader.  Kiah knelt, ran his gloved hand over the dead orc’s armor. Studied his face.  ‘Something is different about this one, something’s not quite right.  He seems almost semi-human.’  Kiah stood staring.  The water around the dead had turned red from the orc blood.  ‘How much blood would be shed along this river in the coming days or even ​​years?’ 

 

“Moss take three riders home then bring back additional horses to help carry the valuables,” Teddy ordered.   

 

“On our way,” Moss replied.   

 

“Wulf, you are reckless,” Bull bellowed approvingly along with a slap on the back.  

 

Wulf nodded towards Bull and gave him a slight, almost embarrassed smile.  Wulf cradled Maggie’s head in his hands then stroked her ​​neck.  He whispered something into her ear, and she replied with a hushed whinny.   

 

The remaining riders were speechless not because they doubted Teddy’s decision to bring Wulf. which they did, because of the young Blackhawk’s prowess.  The former ​​soldiers stared at Wulf in disbelief and simply shook their heads without uttering a word.  Kiah watched their reaction then laughed to himself.  ‘The boy showed no fear.’ 

 

​​​Around mid-afternoon the riders entered Shallow Cross each going their separate ways, each with tales to tell about their adventure along the Border Lands.  Teddy and Wulf rode across the Kinnereth River bridge arriving home a few minutes later.  Despite the cold, Helen and Seth waited for them on the front porch. 

 

“Well, here we go,” Teddy expressed with a slight smile while looking at his son.  “There’s going to be hell to pay.” 

 

Wulf returned the smile cautiously as he watched his mother running towards him.

Chapter Ten (Fall 528, Age of the Arch Mage) 

 

     Teddy guided Kicker north over the Harvest Pike, which was nothing more than a cattle trail. As the ranger, Kiah rode to his brother’s left while the other riders formed a typical cavalry traveling column, two by two. 

     William and his companions seethed as the riders disappeared around a small bend. William spun to face them, his cheeks flushed in anger. He gritted his teeth and spat. “This does not end here.” 

     Carried on the north wind, the flurries began falling a mile into the woodlands. In a matter of a few minutes, the flakes carpeted the countryside in a thick heavy snow, resembling a lumpy homemade bedspread used to keep the winter chill at bay. Teddy flashed a contented smile at his son. “Need more of this, Wulf. Quiets the hooves.” 

     Wulf cracked a thin smile, wishing the orcs had raided Fairbriar during the spring.  

     Onward they trekked, traversing the trail leading into Blackhawks’ northern hill country. Armor and saddles moaned, sword sheaths clanked, buckles and snaps clinked, hooves thumped in harsh rhythm, and men and mounts exhaled clouds of silvery breath in the icy morning air. The bite of the wind heightened their trepidation, whittled at their courage. They barely spoke, if at all, traveling in an apprehensive hush, each veteran recalling previous occasions when they rode into a fight. For some, the battle at the Mulford River. For others, a skirmish along the Border Lands. 

     Kiah held up his hand where the Harvest Pike intersected the Fairbriar Path. He slid from the saddle and knelt in the dirt and snow. He studied the boot prints, the indentations deep and fresh. His finger traced a set of tracks. Loaded down and moving slow. Kiah closed his eyes, freeing his senses to stretch out. No one moved or spoke, their attention focused on the ranger. Kiah stood and wiped the snow and dirt from his hands onto his pants. “Forty orcs. We are two miles behind.” 

     A mile farther on, Kiah stopped the patrol near the base of a rise. Grinning, Kiah gaped at Wulf. “You coming or not?” 

    Wulf shot Kiah a silly grin and dismounted. Kiah and Wulf stalked through a swath of sparse pines toward the top. Halfway to the top, Kiah stopped, an odd expression sweeping over his face. “What’s jingling? Are you wearing chainmail under your leather armor?” 

     “Yes,” Wulf admitted, avoiding his uncle’s amused gaze. 

     “Your mom?” Kiah asked, repressing a laugh. I will have to teach him how to wear it. 

     “Yeah,” Wulf muttered. 

     “Ah. Well, moms are like that,” Kiah confirmed. He gave Wulf an understanding grin and a reassuring nod. “Come on.” 

     The two Blackhawks resumed their climb. Kiah dropped to his belly a few feet from the summit and crawled. Wulf followed his uncle’s lead. From the summit, they viewed the river basin below. Kiah closed his eyes and allowed his senses to stretch out anew. The orcs he tracked advanced towards the North River. He relaxed and cast his senses out farther into the Border Lands. Wulf held his breath and lay motionless. For several long moments, Kiah concentrated on seeking additional threats. Nothing. Kiah shimmied from the rise. “Just the forty. I did not detect any others.” 

     Kiah and Wulf hastened their pace back to their companions and hoisted themselves into the saddle. Kiah rubbed the back of his neck and the grave smile he wore revealed his concern to the other riders. “Forty orcs progressing along the riverbanks.” 

     Teddy winked, acknowledging Kiah’s report. Teddy urged his horse forward and the others followed his lead. Out of the hill country they hastened, thirteen riders determined to intercept the orcs before they escaped into the Border Lands. Stunted oaks and knotted brush welcomed the riders as they cantered into the North River basin. 

     Clad in a crude patchwork of hide and leather armor, the orcs came into full view short of the river’s south bank. Half carried short bows, the balance a large gunny sack slung across their shoulders. Orcs ambled, hunched and bent, their posture resembling an old man in need of a cane. They suffered hoggish faces and low, sloped brows, mirroring the hideous creature from a child’s nightmare. Grotesque or misshapen more aptly defined their appearance. 

     As the terrain broadened, the riders transitioned from a traveling column into a flying wedge, Teddy leading from the center. Wulf rode at his father’s immediate right, allowing Teddy’s sword arm, if needed, to protect his son. Bull maneuvered to Teddy’s other side, prepared to lead the left wing of the attack. Teddy gestured in approval, acknowledging Bull’s continued employment of Crawsterian cavalry tactics. Ever the ranger, Kiah positioned himself at the end of the left flank to support the charge using his bow. 

     As the orcs stepped into the North River, they heard horses’ hooves pounding the frozen ground. Orcs at the rear of the formation shouted a warning, alerting the rest. The orcs dropped the sacks and turned to steel themselves for the looming threat. Some drew short swords while others fitted arrows into short bows. 

     Teddy slowed his horse to some extent, stunned at the boldness of these orcs. No orcs he’d encountered in these parts ever stood to fight as riders galloped at them—they always broke and fled. Teddy was further startled by one acting as a leader and directing the orcs into defensive positions. 

     Kiah recognized what seemed to be a defensive formation employed during the Goblin Wars. He reined Western Union hard to the left, crossing the North to deter any single orc from fleeing. Teddy grinned as Kiah splashed into the river. He noticed the same thing.

     Teddy started the attack by releasing the arrow nocked in his longbow. The arrow struck an orc, piercing its leather armor. The orc died a few yards short of the river. 

     One down. 

     Wulf let an arrow fly. 

     Two down. 

     The riders along the south bank fired their arrows, killing more orcs in the river’s shallow waters. 

     With a ruthless swiftness, the riders halved the number of orcs. Several of the orcs panicked, despite the frantic urgings of their leader. Desperate shrieks burst from their throats, legs became entangled, and clawed hands dropped swords and bows. Every direction the orcs turned, arrows rained down on them. 

     Kiah, Bull, and the riders along the north bank unleashed a volley of arrows that cut down more orcs, trapping the surviving between the two riverbanks. 

     The orc leader stood a head above the other orcs. It remained calm despite the carnage, as if this were not its first skirmish fighting men. Sneering, it took aim. Its arrow found the intended target—Teddy and his mount tumbled into the river. Screaming in satisfaction, the orc leader placed another arrow into the short bow. 

     Wulf observed his father and horse plunge into the icy water. There was no thought or hesitation, Wulf urged Maggie into the river. 

     “Wulf, no!” Kiah and Bull shouted in unison. 

     Wulf yanked Ill Wind free from its scabbard and welcomed the heft of the ancient blade in his hands. Maggie surged forward, her gait steady and smooth, a furious charge straight for the large orc. It stood in defiance and cursed. Who was this mere man-child? The orc scoffed, aiming its bow at Wulf. Its arrow grazed the side of Wulf’s neck above the shoulder pad, drawing blood. The slicing wound forced a grimace. In an instant, the pain from the arrow replaced the shock of Maggie hitting the freezing river in full stride. 

     Wulf shrugged off the discomfort, urging Maggie forward, faster. He closed the distance before the orc could fire again. Nearing his target, Wulf launched himself into the leader, the impact vicious, bloody and savage. His shoulder shattered the orc’s chest, breaking bone and driving air from the orc’s lungs. The violent collision sent both into the shallow river. 

     Stunned, the orc floundered on its back, gasping for breath, groping for its sword. The orc’s arms and legs churned the water as it struggled to recover. 

     Wulf gained his feet and gathered himself into a crouch. As he had learned in years of training, he held his sword out in front of his body. Gripping Ill Wind, he scanned the battle. His muscles tensed, his eyes wide and focused. He felt the frigid water trickling down his face, felt his heart hammering inside his temples. Silence fell about him. All he could hear was the sound of his own breathing. 

     In a split-second, Wulf raised his sword. He drove the blade deep into the orc leader’s chest, ending any chance for recovery. He removed Ill Wind from the dead orc, then pivoted to face the other assailants. 

     The last handful of orcs rallied to their fallen leader. Snarling and growling, they approached Wulf. Arrows from the riders dropped three, leaving two alive. The two left pressed their attack. 

     “Strong, but clumsy,” Wulf mouthed. “Move fast. Strike hard. And show no fear.” 

     Teddy came out of the water spitting and coughing. He grabbed a handful of mane and used Kicker to gain his feet. He wiped away the water clouding his vision. The scene churning around him was chaotic as his riders pressed their attack. Bull and Moss had dismounted, their swords cutting a swath through the orcs. Dane and Franklin remained astride, their bows purring as arrows leapt from the string. The shouting, the wounded’s cries, and the smell of death hung over the river.  

     Teddy had endured combat’s horror before, many times. His hands trembled as he fumbled to pull an arrow from his quiver. Wet and numb fingers worked against him as he armed his bow. Teddy felt his heart race, surveying the river and the banks for Wulf. A dozen yards to the east, he located his son battling two orcs. Blast! He drew the bowstring tight to his cheek.

Wulf shifted his weight on to his right foot and readied himself for the second orc to strike. The second orc over-committed, its errant sword thrust missing wide. Ill Wind exploded in a blinding flash of steel, cleaving flesh and severing ribs. Clutching its midsection, the orc crumpled dead into the river. The first orc recovered and attacked. Wulf spun to meet the assault. The orc readied a strike, its short sword lifted above its head. Wulf hoisted his sword above his shoulders to block the blow, which did not come. 

     Teddy and Kiah released their arrows at the same time. Their missiles streaked through the falling snow, piercing the back of the orc’s skull before it could complete the swing. Teddy leaned into Kicker and exhaled, relieved the fight was done.

Throwing her head, she plowed into the water, coming to Wulf’s side. Wulf cradled her head in his hands, then stroked Maggie’s neck. He whispered words meant for her, and she whinnied in response.

Kiah knelt, passed his gloved hand over the dead orc’s armor, studying his face. Something is unusual here. Something not quite. He is a half-orc. Kiah shuddered at the notion and stood staring, his expression hard to read. Emotions drained away as his thoughts dwelled upon the dead orc. Orc blood and riverbed silt transformed the water’s turquoise hue near the dead into a blend of crimson and russet. A grim premonition marred the ranger’s face. We will shed a lot of blood in this river during the coming days. 

     “Moss, take Dane and Franklin home and bring back additional horses to help carry the valuables,” Teddy ordered. 

     “On our way,” Moss answered. 

     “The rest of you gather the sacks of plunder,” Teddy barked. 

     Teddy lingered beside the river, eager to hear what Kiah had discovered. Teddy saw the concern in his brother’s eyes, knew Kiah uncovered something worrisome. 

    “The others appear to be your average orcs,” Kiah explained, still frowning. “Wulf killed a half-orc.” 

     Teddy shook his head. There was no need to speak. Half-orcs led other orcs into battle, not on raids. Kiah scanned the basin another time, then a final wary gaze north. 

     “Are they testing our defenses?” Teddy wondered. 

     “I don’t know,” Kiah mumbled, his expression mirroring the bleak weather. He peered at this brother. “You with me?” 

     Teddy hesitated ahead of answering. He looked at Kiah and was proud they were brothers and best friends. Teddy put his arm over Kiah’s shoulder. “To the end.”

    The riders entered Shallow Cross, weary and chilled, by midafternoon. Each went their separate ways, each owning tales to tell relating to their adventure along the Border Lands. 

     “Say ‘hi’ to Helen for me,” Kiah snickered as he rode off. 

     “Why don’t you tell her yourself?” Teddy shot back. “Afraid?” 

     “Absolutely,” Kiah shouted over his shoulder. 

     “Blast,” Teddy muttered. 

     Teddy and Wulf shivered in the falling snow, each spent from the day’s fight. Over the Kinnereth River bridge they rode, each longing for warm clothes and several hours in front of the fire, nursing a mug of coffee. A stiff breeze lashed at them and slowed the passing minutes to a crawl before Teddy and Wulf arrived home. Bundled in their heavy winter cloaks, Helen and Seth waited for them on the front porch. 

     “Well, here we go,” Teddy moaned, his expression strained and apprehensive. “There’s going to be hell to pay.”
